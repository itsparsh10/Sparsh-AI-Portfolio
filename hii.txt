Example:

Challenge

Running the application from external storage introduced read/write constraints for runtime databases.

Approach

Separated immutable application assets from writable runtime data and changed initialization around the writable location.

Result

The application can initialize its runtime state without modifying the packaged application files.

THE VISUAL LANGUAGE

Now, when you say "Silicon Valley / YC premium", don't interpret that as:

❌ Gradients everywhere
❌ Glassmorphism everywhere
❌ 3D objects everywhere
❌ 30 animations
❌ Huge glowing text
❌ Excessive rounded cards

Instead:

1. Typography

Huge, confident headlines.

Something like:

The intelligence layer for personal computing.

Not 4 paragraphs.

2. Massive whitespace

Give important things room.

3. Real product UI

This is probably the single biggest thing.

Your product screenshots are your marketing.

4. Restrained color

One project-specific accent.

RIGEL could use your existing orange identity.

A RAG project might use a different accent.

5. Motion

Use motion to explain:

scroll
   ↓
architecture node appears
   ↓
connection draws
   ↓
product screenshot transitions

Not:

Everything flies around because animation looks cool.

6. Progressive disclosure

First:

What is it?

Then:

Why does it matter?

Then:

How does it work?

Then:

How did you build it?

Then:

Can I see it?

That's the right information hierarchy.

THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE

YC's design review makes a particularly useful point: don't make the visitor work to figure out what you're showing them. The review specifically focuses on whether the intended product pitch comes through quickly and whether the site gets visitors to the product/demo.

So your project page should pass this test:

At 5 seconds:

Do I know what this is?

At 20 seconds:

Do I know why it exists?

At 45 seconds:

Do I understand the major features?

At 90 seconds:

Do I understand how it works?

At 2–3 minutes:

Do I believe this person actually engineered it?

At the end:

Can I see the code/demo immediately?

If yes → you've built the right page.
